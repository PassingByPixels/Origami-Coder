Walk the LINK GRAPH of this workspace's knowledge base out from one page: what that page links
to, what links TO it, and which pages share its tags.

WHEN TO USE IT: after wiki_search names a page, or whenever you need the neighbourhood of a
topic rather than a single page - the surrounding decisions, the pages that cite this one, the
siblings filed under the same tags.

INBOUND LINKS ARE THE POINT. "Which pages point at this one" is the question grep cannot answer
cheaply, because a link is written as a bare name while the page lives at a path - and the
context you are missing is usually in the page that cites yours, not in the page itself.
Outbound targets with no page behind them are marked `(unwritten)`: those are known gaps in the
wiki, not broken output.

WHAT COMES BACK: METADATA ONLY - page ids, tags, one-line descriptions. No page bodies. Follow
up by READING the one or two best neighbours with the read tool.

`page` takes an exact page id, a bare filename, or an approximate name - the reply always says
how it resolved, and an unresolvable name comes back as the nearest page ids rather than an
error. `depth: 2` adds one more hop on the outbound and inbound sets, indented, under the same
output cap.
